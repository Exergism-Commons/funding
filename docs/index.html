Anti-capture architecture

Capital without surrendering the institution.

Funding is useful only if it leaves EC with more durable agency than before. These safeguards make dependence visible and external control expensive.

01

No governance-for-money

Funding does not buy votes, board seats, vetoes, roadmap control or unrelated institutional positions.

02

IP firewall

Pre-existing EC assets remain under EC or project governance. Exclusive control is treated as high capture risk.

03

Diversification by design

A single funder above 30% of rolling 24-month income triggers diversification; above 50% is strategic dependency.

04

Exitability

EC must be able to refuse renewal without losing identity, domains, core repositories, governance or essential infrastructure.

Portfolio model

Multiple engines. No single lifeline.

The desired portfolio mixes restricted grants with flexible and earned revenue so that one programme never becomes synonymous with EC's survival.

R&I

European research

Horizon Europe, MSCA and adjacent programmes: research capacity, staff, publications and consortium access.

FOSS

Open-technology grants

NLnet and comparable funders: sharply scoped infrastructure with high autonomy value and reusable outputs.

NET

Strategic networks

COST, Digital Commons EDIC and allied institutions: relationships, legitimacy, standards and early consortium formation.

CORE

Unrestricted base

Memberships, donations, sponsorships, training and mission-aligned services: the capital that lets EC decline bad deals.

RES

Reserves

Progress from three to six to twelve months of operating runway, without pretending restricted grant money is free cash.
