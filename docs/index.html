Exergism Commons · institutional capacity

Funding that increases freedom to act.

Exergism Commons does not optimise for the largest grant. It builds a diversified portfolio of capital, capability, infrastructure, legitimacy and networks — and converts part of that success into institutionally owned permanent capital.

02 · Anti-capture architecture

Capital without surrendering the institution.

Funding is useful only if it leaves EC with more durable agency than before. These safeguards make dependence visible and external control expensive.

01

No governance-for-money

Funding does not buy votes, board seats, vetoes, roadmap control or unrelated institutional positions.

02

IP firewall

Pre-existing EC assets remain under EC or project governance. Exclusive control is treated as high capture risk.

03

Diversification by design

A single funder above 30% of rolling 24-month income triggers diversification; above 50% is strategic dependency.

04

No private ownership

Membership never becomes an economic share in EC income, reserves, Endowment principal or investment returns.

03 · Capital model

Multiple engines. No single lifeline.

The desired architecture mixes external funding, flexible income, liquid resilience and permanent capital so that no programme becomes synonymous with EC's survival.

R&I

European research

Horizon Europe, MSCA and adjacent programmes: research capacity, staff, publications and consortium access.

FOSS

Open-technology grants

NLnet and comparable funders: sharply scoped infrastructure with high autonomy value and reusable outputs.

CORE

Unrestricted base

Memberships, donations, sponsorships, training and mission-aligned services: flexible capital that lets EC decline bad deals.

RES

Strategic Reserve

Progress from three to six to twelve months of operating runway, without pretending restricted grant money is free cash.

END

EC Endowment

Long-duration capital owned by EC, intended to compound and finance an increasing share of future mission expenditure.

04 · Permanent capital

The EC Endowment.

A long-term pool of institutional capital designed to make EC progressively less dependent on grants, donors, sponsors, customers and political cycles.

CAPITAL

Owned by EC

Donors, founders and members do not receive repayment, investment returns, residual ownership or governance rights merely because capital enters the Endowment.

SPENDING

Preserve the principal

Mature-state ordinary distributions target up to 4% of a trailing multi-year Endowment value; principal is presumptively permanent institutional capital.

FLOW

Compound successful years

After adequate operating liquidity and reserves, part of unrestricted surplus should be capitalised instead of automatically becoming recurring expenditure.

PEOPLE

Pay work, not ownership

Members may be compensated for genuine work under EC policy and applicable law. Compensation never becomes a dividend or pro-rata claim on Endowment returns.
