# Exergism Commons Endowment Policy

## Purpose

The **Exergism Commons Endowment (EC Endowment)** is a long-term pool of institutional capital owned by Exergism Commons (EC) and governed for the durable financing of EC's mission.

Its purpose is not to maximise financial return. Its purpose is to convert part of EC's present unrestricted capacity into **future autonomous capacity** so that EC becomes progressively less dependent on any particular grant, donor, sponsor, customer or political cycle.

The Endowment is not a collective investment product and does not create economic ownership rights for contributors, donors, members or funders. It is institutional property of EC, subject to applicable law, tax rules, accounting requirements and EC governance.

## 1. Core principle

**Capital belongs to EC; membership does not create a claim on that capital.**

A contribution to the Endowment does not entitle any person or organisation to:

- repayment;
- a share of investment returns;
- a residual claim on EC assets;
- governance rights;
- preferential access to EC resources;
- control over investment policy;
- control over EC's general programme or mission.

Any restricted gift or legally binding condition must be separately documented and must pass EC's anti-capture review.

## 2. Sources of Endowment capital

Subject to legal and contractual restrictions, Endowment capital may come from:

- unrestricted donations and bequests;
- unrestricted sponsorship;
- unrestricted operating surpluses;
- mission-aligned earned-income surpluses;
- specific endowment gifts accepted under EC governance;
- investment returns retained within the Endowment;
- other lawful unrestricted resources designated by EC governance.

Restricted project funding must not be transferred into the Endowment unless the applicable agreement expressly permits it.

## 3. Capital-preservation rule

The Endowment principal is presumptively **permanent institutional capital**.

Ordinary operations should be financed from operating income, operating reserves and the Endowment spending distribution rather than by consuming Endowment principal.

A draw on principal should require an exceptional governance decision, a documented justification and an assessment of the effect on long-term institutional independence.

Examples of potentially legitimate exceptional uses include:

- existential continuity of EC;
- protection of essential institutional assets;
- extraordinary strategic opportunities whose expected institutional value clearly exceeds the loss of permanent capital;
- legal or regulatory obligations that cannot reasonably be met otherwise.

## 4. Spending rule

The Endowment should support EC through a predictable annual distribution rather than ad-hoc withdrawals.

Mature-state target:

- ordinary annual distribution: **up to 4%** of a trailing multi-year average Endowment value;
- governance may approve a lower distribution when preservation or compounding has greater strategic value;
- distributions above the ordinary ceiling require explicit exceptional justification.

Until enough historical valuation data exists to calculate a robust trailing average, EC governance should approve distributions conservatively and document the methodology used.

The 4% figure is a governance target, not a guaranteed return assumption and not a statement of investment performance.

## 5. Compounding objective

A substantial part of investment returns should ordinarily remain invested.

The long-run objective is for the Endowment to grow faster than EC's spending requirement after inflation, taxes, fees and distributions, so that its real financing capacity is preserved or increased over time.

## 6. Separation from operating liquidity

The Endowment must not be treated as EC's current-account balance.

EC should maintain three conceptually distinct pools:

1. **Operating Treasury** — cash and near-cash required for current obligations.
2. **Strategic Reserve** — liquid resilience capital for medium-term uncertainty and continuity.
3. **EC Endowment** — long-duration institutional capital intended to compound and provide recurring mission funding.

See `treasury-strategy.md` for the liquidity architecture.

## 7. Investment principles

Subject to applicable law and a future detailed Investment Policy Statement, the Endowment should favour:

- broad diversification;
- low-cost implementation;
- high transparency;
- adequate liquidity relative to the spending rule;
- avoidance of unnecessary leverage;
- avoidance of concentrated speculative positions;
- operational simplicity appropriate to EC's institutional capacity;
- custody and counterparties appropriate to a non-profit institution;
- risk levels consistent with a long investment horizon.

The Endowment should not be used as a vehicle for discretionary speculation by individual members or officers.

A detailed asset-allocation policy should be adopted only when EC has sufficient capital and administrative capacity to manage it responsibly.

## 8. Anti-capture protections

No donor, sponsor, investment provider or external manager obtains governance authority merely because they contributed capital or manage assets.

EC should avoid arrangements that create:

- donor vetoes over general governance;
- permanent exclusivity obligations;
- proprietary rights over EC-wide infrastructure unrelated to a specific lawful agreement;
- dependency on a single financial institution without an exit path;
- investment conditions that distort EC's mission.

Material Endowment gifts and restrictions should be publicly disclosed where legally and practically possible.

## 9. Compensation firewall

The Endowment does not create a distributable economic interest for EC members.

Members, officers and contributors may be compensated for genuine work or services performed for EC under applicable law and EC's compensation policy, but compensation must not be calculated as a dividend, ownership distribution or pro-rata share of Endowment capital or investment returns.

Endowment distributions flow first to **EC**, and EC then allocates its budget according to governance, contracts, legal obligations and mission priorities.

See `compensation-principles.md`.

## 10. Governance and conflicts of interest

Material Endowment decisions should record:

- the decision-maker;
- investment or spending rationale;
- relevant risks;
- conflicts of interest;
- fees and counterparties where material;
- any restrictions attached to donated capital;
- exceptional principal withdrawals.

A person with a material personal interest in an investment, counterparty or compensation decision should disclose that interest and follow the applicable recusal/conflict procedure.

## 11. Transparency

At an appropriate level of aggregation, EC should aim to publish:

- Endowment market value;
- annual contributions;
- annual distributions;
- investment return net of material fees where practical;
- high-level asset allocation;
- exceptional principal withdrawals;
- restricted versus unrestricted Endowment components where relevant.

Publication must not expose banking credentials, account identifiers, security-sensitive data or protected personal information.

## 12. Long-term objective

The strategic objective is not merely to build a large portfolio.

It is to reach a state in which a growing share of EC's core research, infrastructure, contributor time and public-interest work can be financed from capital that **EC already owns and controls**.

**The Endowment is successful when EC can continue important work even when external funders say no.**

## Legal and tax note

This policy is an internal governance design, not legal, tax or investment advice. Implementation must be reviewed against the law, tax status, accounting rules, statutes and regulatory obligations applicable to EC at the relevant time.
