# Funding Strategy

## Purpose

Exergism Commons (EC) should use funding to accumulate **autonomous institutional capacity** rather than maximise short-term grant income.

Funding is valuable when it increases one or more of the following without disproportionate loss of autonomy:

- deployable capital;
- paid contributor/researcher time;
- technical infrastructure;
- durable knowledge and reusable artefacts;
- legal and administrative capacity;
- scientific and institutional legitimacy;
- access to networks, consortia and public institutions;
- recurring revenue;
- strategic reserves.

## Portfolio principle

EC should maintain a deliberately heterogeneous portfolio. A healthy mature mix may include:

1. **Open-source/digital-commons grants** — relatively autonomous project funding.
2. **European R&I** — Horizon Europe and MSCA for research capacity and institutional integration.
3. **European deployment/network programmes** — Digital Europe, COST and adjacent instruments.
4. **National/regional public funding** — Spain, autonomous communities and public agencies.
5. **Philanthropy/foundations** — where mission and independence conditions are acceptable.
6. **Institutional sponsorship/membership** — preferably recurring and non-governing.
7. **Community funding** — donations and recurring small sponsorships.
8. **Earned income** — contracts, training, audits, support and other mission-aligned services.
9. **Reserves/endowment-like assets** — long-term financial resilience, subject to EC governance and applicable law.

## Institutional value model

Grant size alone is a poor ranking function. Each opportunity should be evaluated across:

- `fit`: alignment with EC mission and existing work;
- `funding_value`: expected direct financial contribution;
- `autonomy_value`: freedom created by the funding;
- `capture_risk`: funder leverage over governance, roadmap, IP or public positions;
- `admin_cost`: proposal, reporting, audit and coordination burden;
- `network_value`: quality of relationships created;
- `capability_value`: durable capability remaining after the grant ends;
- `recurrence`: probability of repeat/follow-on funding;
- `strategic_optionality`: doors opened by successful participation;
- `execution_risk`: probability that obligations consume more capacity than they create.

A future scoring tool may compute an **Expected Institutional Value (EIV)** instead of ranking calls by headline budget.

## Phased strategy

### Phase 0 — Formation

Targets:

- establish legal personality and required registrations;
- obtain EU Participant Identification Code (PIC) when appropriate;
- document governance and financial controls;
- create public funding intelligence;
- secure first small independent grants;
- begin recurring community/sponsor revenue.

### Phase 1 — First financed capacity

Indicative annual scale: **EUR 50k–150k**.

Preferred structure:

- one or more open-source grants;
- a modest role in a consortium;
- recurring sponsorship/community revenue;
- limited mission-aligned earned income.

Goal: finance real contributor time without making survival dependent on one programme.

### Phase 2 — Research organisation

Indicative annual scale: **EUR 150k–400k**.

Possible components:

- MSCA-hosted researcher;
- Horizon beneficiary work package;
- open-source foundation grants;
- contracts/sponsorship;
- growing unrestricted reserve.

Goal: sustain 1–3 paid people and durable research/technical operations.

### Phase 3 — Independent institution

Indicative annual scale: **EUR 500k–1m+** from multiple independent sources.

Goals:

- 6+ meaningful funding sources/categories;
- 6–12 months operating reserve;
- meaningful unrestricted/core revenue;
- ability to reject misaligned grants;
- ability to issue microgrants/fellowships or otherwise fund external contributors;
- capacity to lead work packages and eventually coordinate selected programmes.

## Networking as capital

EC should treat relationships as an explicit output of funding activity.

Priority networks include:

- Digital Commons EDIC;
- European Commission and programme agencies;
- universities and research organisations;
- open-source foundations and maintainers;
- public administrations;
- standards/semantic-web communities;
- data-space and digital-governance consortia;
- aligned civil-society organisations.

For some opportunities, network value may exceed the immediate grant amount.

## Operating rule

**Funding must adapt to EC. EC must not be reshaped merely to fit funding.**

A call that requires EC to abandon its mission, surrender IP/governance control, manufacture irrelevant deliverables, or become structurally dependent should be rejected even if financially attractive.
