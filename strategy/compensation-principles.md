# Compensation Principles

## Purpose

Exergism Commons (EC) may need to compensate members, officers, researchers, developers, administrators and other contributors for real work performed for the organisation.

This policy separates **compensation for work** from **economic rights arising from membership**.

The central rule is simple:

**Membership creates governance participation according to EC's rules; it does not create a right to receive a share of EC income, surplus, reserves or Endowment capital.**

## 1. Work may be compensated

Subject to applicable law, EC governance and available funding, a member may be paid for genuine work or services performed for EC.

Examples include:

- research;
- software development;
- project management;
- administration;
- fundraising;
- communications;
- legal/technical coordination;
- infrastructure operations;
- teaching or training;
- other mission-aligned work.

The relevant legal relationship may differ by jurisdiction and circumstances and must be structured correctly.

## 2. Membership is not an economic share

EC should never describe compensation as a member's "share" of revenue or surplus.

A member is not entitled to:

- a percentage of annual profit or surplus merely because they are a member;
- a pro-rata share of Endowment returns;
- a distribution of EC reserves;
- a residual ownership claim on EC assets;
- automatic remuneration merely because another member is remunerated.

Budget surplus remains institutional capital unless validly spent for EC's purposes.

## 3. Equal-pay models are permitted as governance choices

EC may adopt an equal or highly compressed base-pay model when it considers that structure consistent with its mission and applicable law.

For example, EC may decide that people with equivalent full-time commitment receive the same base compensation regardless of bargaining power or governance title, with pro-rata adjustment for documented time commitment.

Illustrative model:

- 1.0 FTE -> 100% of common base compensation;
- 0.5 FTE -> 50%;
- 0.25 FTE -> 25%.

This is a governance model, not a requirement. Role complexity, legal employment categories, market constraints, grants and other legitimate factors may require different treatment.

## 4. Compensation must have a real basis

Every material compensation arrangement should identify:

- the work or role performed;
- expected time commitment or deliverables;
- compensation basis;
- duration;
- funding source where relevant;
- applicable employment, contractor or other legal status;
- approval route;
- conflicts of interest.

Calling a distribution "salary" does not make it compensation if no real work or legal basis exists.

## 5. Governance-role compensation versus operational work

Where a person both holds an EC governance office and performs operational work, the two functions should be distinguished.

Examples:

- governance role: president, secretary, treasurer or board member;
- operational role: researcher, software engineer, project manager or administrator.

Compensation for governance office and compensation for separate professional work may be subject to different statutory, tax, grant or conflict-of-interest rules.

EC should document which function is being compensated.

## 6. Conflict-of-interest rule

A person should not have unchecked discretion to set their own compensation.

Where a decision affects the compensation of one or more decision-makers, EC should use safeguards proportionate to its size, such as:

- disclosure of the conflict;
- recusal where legally or practically possible;
- a pre-approved compensation formula or salary band;
- external salary benchmarking;
- written justification in meeting minutes;
- budget approval separated from individual payment execution;
- independent review when EC has enough members or external advisers to make that practical.

In EC's earliest stage, where all active members may also be workers, universal conflict cannot always be eliminated. In that case the response should be **more objective process and documentation**, not pretending the conflict does not exist.

## 7. No disguised surplus distribution

The following patterns should be treated as red flags:

- dividing year-end surplus equally among members without reference to work;
- paying members in proportion to membership or voting power;
- increasing compensation solely because unrestricted surplus happens to be available;
- draining reserves or Endowment principal to create member payouts without a genuine mission/work basis;
- retroactively inventing services to justify a distribution.

Such arrangements may undermine EC's non-profit character and require legal review.

## 8. Compensation and the Endowment

Endowment income belongs to EC.

A valid flow is:

**Endowment -> EC budget -> approved mission expenditure -> compensation for genuine work.**

The following is not an EC policy objective:

**Endowment -> automatic percentage distribution to members.**

No member should receive an ownership-like claim over Endowment performance.

## 9. Funding-source neutrality

Where practical, EC should avoid allowing a particular funder to determine the general compensation hierarchy of the organisation.

Grant-funded roles may have programme-specific cost rules, but EC should maintain its own compensation framework so that external programmes do not silently become internal governance.

## 10. Transparency

EC should aim to publish an appropriate level of information about paid work, such as:

- compensation policy;
- salary bands or common-base methodology;
- aggregate personnel expenditure;
- governance-role remuneration where disclosure is legally required or institutionally appropriate;
- related-party/conflict procedures.

Exact individual compensation may be disclosed when required or deliberately chosen, but privacy and employment-law considerations should be respected.

## 11. Strategic principle

**People should be able to earn a living by doing EC's work without acquiring a private ownership claim over EC itself.**

This allows EC to professionalise while preserving the distinction between a commons institution and a profit-distributing company.

## Legal note

Compensation arrangements must comply with EC's statutes, association law, employment/self-employment rules, tax and social-security obligations, grant conditions and any public-benefit regime applicable at the time. This document sets governance principles and is not legal or tax advice.
