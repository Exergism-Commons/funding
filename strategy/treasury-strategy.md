# Treasury and Capital Allocation Strategy

## Purpose

Exergism Commons (EC) should manage money according to **time horizon and institutional purpose**, rather than holding every euro in one undifferentiated pool.

The treasury architecture exists to balance four needs:

- meet current obligations reliably;
- survive funding interruptions;
- preserve strategic flexibility;
- compound permanent institutional capital.

## 1. Three-pool architecture

### A. Operating Treasury

Purpose: current and near-term obligations.

Typical uses:

- payroll and contractor payments;
- taxes and social-security obligations;
- hosting, domains and software;
- rent, insurance and administration;
- approved project expenditure;
- near-term grant co-financing or reimbursement gaps.

Operating Treasury should prioritise liquidity, capital stability and operational access over investment return.

### B. Strategic Reserve

Purpose: resilience and optionality.

Target progression:

1. three months of core operating expenses;
2. six months;
3. twelve months as a mature resilience target.

The Strategic Reserve is not the Endowment. It exists to be usable when revenue is delayed, a grant ends, a contract fails, or EC deliberately rejects misaligned funding.

Restricted project cash must not be counted as unrestricted reserve.

### C. EC Endowment

Purpose: permanent or very long-duration capital that compounds and supports future EC activity through a controlled spending distribution.

The Endowment should normally be invested with a longer horizon and should not be used to solve routine cash-flow problems.

See `endowment-policy.md`.

## 2. Capital-allocation waterfall

When unrestricted cash is available, EC should normally consider uses in the following order:

1. satisfy legal, tax, payroll and contractual obligations;
2. maintain adequate near-term operating liquidity;
3. repair any deficit in the minimum Strategic Reserve;
4. fund approved mission-critical investment in institutional capacity;
5. allocate a meaningful share of remaining unrestricted surplus to the EC Endowment;
6. retain additional strategic liquidity when upcoming risks or opportunities justify it.

This is a decision framework, not an automatic accounting rule.

## 3. Endowment contribution discipline

During EC's bootstrap stage, no fixed contribution percentage is required because liquidity may be more valuable than long-duration capital.

Once EC has achieved a six-month unrestricted reserve, there should be a **presumption in favour of capitalising part of annual unrestricted surplus**.

Mature-state target:

- ordinarily consider allocating **at least 25% of unrestricted annual surplus** to the Endowment unless governance documents a stronger use of funds;
- larger allocations are encouraged when EC has excess liquidity and limited near-term deployment needs;
- the percentage should be reviewed annually against runway, growth opportunities, legal constraints and expected funding volatility.

The purpose of this rule is to prevent every successful year from being converted entirely into higher recurring expenditure.

## 4. Restricted versus unrestricted funds

Treasury reporting must clearly distinguish:

- restricted grant/project funds;
- unrestricted operating funds;
- designated Strategic Reserve;
- Endowment principal;
- Endowment distributions available for spending.

Money cannot be made unrestricted merely by moving it between bank or investment accounts.

## 5. Liquidity risk

EC should avoid situations where it owns assets but cannot meet obligations when due.

Before moving capital from Treasury or Reserve into the Endowment, governance should consider:

- payroll runway;
- grant reimbursement delays;
- tax liabilities;
- contractual commitments;
- expected capital expenditure;
- concentration of incoming revenue;
- probability of losing a major funding source;
- realistic liquidation time and costs.

## 6. Currency and counterparty risk

As EC grows, treasury reporting should identify material exposure to:

- non-EUR currencies;
- individual banks or brokers;
- money-market or debt counterparties;
- payment processors;
- custodians;
- concentration in a single financial institution.

No counterparty should become an avoidable single point of institutional failure.

## 7. No speculative treasury

Operating cash and Strategic Reserve should not be used for speculative trading.

The treasury function exists to preserve EC's ability to operate, not to generate a trading profit.

Higher-volatility assets, if ever permitted, belong under a formally approved Endowment investment policy and must be proportionate to EC's horizon, capacity and risk tolerance.

## 8. Reporting

At minimum, internal treasury reporting should show:

- unrestricted cash;
- restricted cash;
- months of core runway;
- Strategic Reserve balance;
- Endowment value;
- expected receivables and major obligations;
- revenue concentration;
- significant liquidity or counterparty risks.

Public reporting may aggregate these figures where exact disclosure would create security, privacy or negotiating risks.

## 9. Institutional objective

The target state is a financial architecture in which EC can:

- operate without constant emergency fundraising;
- reject a problematic grant without immediate collapse;
- finance periods of independent research;
- invest in contributors and infrastructure before a funder authorises the work;
- use external funding as acceleration rather than life support.

**Liquidity protects the present. Reserves protect continuity. The Endowment protects future autonomy.**

## Legal and accounting note

Actual account structures, permissible investments, tax treatment and accounting classification must follow the legal and tax status applicable to EC. This document defines strategic governance intent rather than professional financial, tax or legal advice.
